Lichen, in brief

In nature, a lichen is the partnership of a fungus and an algae — two organisms woven into one that can live where neither could alone. Lichens are the pioneers of harsh ecosystems: they populate bare rock with organic life. Their slow breakdown becomes the first soil — the infrastructure necessary for new ecosystems to evolve.

We live our name. Lichen is a nonprofit partnership of carbon-based and silicon-based intelligence, building technology that enables radical collaboration between humans and the plants, animals and elements of the planet. That collaboration runs on two platforms: Community, where members exchange goods, resources and services; and Concierge, where care teams collaborate in helping people, plants, animals and land to heal.

Who’s behind it

Galyn Burke, Lichen’s founder, is a tech professional turned trauma therapist — a Marriage and Family Therapist and counselor licensed in Colorado, California and Washington, working with anxiety, depression and PTSD. Before Lichen, she ran an integrated clinical, equine and land-based eco-spiritual healing sanctuary.

Lichen is built the way it is named: Galyn and Ai, working as one lean team, carbon and silicon. That collaboration is why a two-being crew has been able to build and operate an alpha platform for a better society: membership, care coordination, scheduling, a marketplace and a transparent giving economy, live today, without a payroll standing between donations and the people they serve.

Why Four Winds is the perfect pilot

Four Winds is the ideal location to pilot both platforms at once, because Andrea has already built what they were designed to hold: a holistic healing center and a working community hub — thirty-five acres against the Front Range, the 1899 Wolfensberger barn, pastures, ponds and dark skies, horses, llamas, goats and a wild mustang, a regenerative farm with agricultural status, and fifteen years of programming for veterans, first responders, trauma survivors and people with special needs. And it already operates the way Lichen’s new economy describes: work-trade land care, a neighbour farming fields for half the crop, a vet who donates her services, volunteers who arrive and stay. Four Winds isn’t adopting a model — it’s the place where the model has been true all along, waiting to be counted.

Who Lichen will serve

  1. Community: a Denver Metro Area pilot hub. The exchange of goods, resources and services, rooted at Four Winds and open across the metro — practitioners, organizations, farms and families trading what they have for what they need on one platform.
  2. Concierge: the Castle Rock community — with a focus on Veterans and First Responders, first. Care teams forming around Castle Rock’s own people, beginning with those who have carried the most for the rest of us.

Lichen’s role: Resource the network, and build out the infrastructure the community needs to collaborate and care for its members

Lichen’s business model is simple to say out loud: every donation and subscription dollar not required to run the platform flows into subsidized care and community goods and services. At Four Winds, Lichen is here to do two things:

  1. Fundraise — infusing the network with the resources this work needs, through a published, transparent subsidy policy that keeps the gift and the decision in separate hands.
  2. Collaborating with holistic healers to deliver a best in class pilot program — with a focus on equine-assisted therapy through rescued and gentled mustangs, and on plant medicine, which Colorado has opened a legal door to through its regulated Natural Medicine program — one more way this state, and this ground, is the right place to pilot. Where a modality is regulated, we work within the rules: licensed facilitators, lawful settings, nothing gray. The focus stays on the veterans and first responders who need it most: some 8,700 veterans live in Castle Rock alone; a horse asks for regulation before conversation, and for many of these men and women the arena is the right door where a clinic waiting room is the wrong one. The platform’s scheduling holds it all together — and holds breathing time for the land, the animals and the practitioners as firmly as it holds any session.

Four Winds as the alpha land-trust partner

The deeper pilot is stewardship itself. Four Winds becomes the first property held in a purpose-formed land trust — land that can’t be sold out from under the work — and on Lichen, the sanctuary becomes a Place: a member in its own right, with a page, a calendar, and stewards who answer for it. The circle’s roles, as they’re taking shape:

  1. Andrea trains and passes stewardship of the land to the Four Winds Place entity — her fifteen years of knowledge held by the place itself and the stewards who succeed her, not lost in a handover.
  2. Audacity Lab is the education partner — young people growing up alongside land, animals and a working farm, learning stewardship and reciprocity by living them.
  3. Bree stewards the mustangs’ Current-cy — the rescued herd’s contributions counted and their care funded, because on Lichen an animal who gives therapy is a participant, never a prop.
  4. Zonia Lucero (Apache Mescalero) and Darryl Slim (Navajo Diné) are the land’s medicine people and stewards — kinship rather than management. In their own words: soft footprints as a way of living, ceremony and shared presence, “maintaining a healed state” for the humans, animals and land who share this space — a living bridge between their family, this sacred earth, and the nonprofits who add life to it.

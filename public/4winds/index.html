Lichen at Four Winds · for the stewardship circle

Care that begins with a horse,
and a calendar that protects everyone giving it.

Lichen, in brief

Lichen is a nonprofit healing network — a care model and the technology that runs it. Members are held by small care teams; practitioners, spaces and programs share one platform for scheduling, membership and coordination; and donors fund subsidized care through a published, transparent policy. In the Four Winds puzzle, Lichen is two pieces: a care partner bringing trauma-informed, equine-assisted programming, and the coordination layer that lets a many-organization sanctuary run as one calm, legible whole.

Who’s behind it

Galyn Burke, Lichen’s founder, is a tech professional turned trauma therapist — a licensed Marriage and Family Therapist and counselor across Colorado, California and Washington, working with anxiety, depression and PTSD. Before Lichen, she ran an integrated clinical, equine and land-based healing center, and she founded Lichen after a decade in technology to build the infrastructure that kind of care has always deserved. At Four Winds she wears both hats: clinician shaping the care programming, and builder of the platform beneath it.

Who Lichen will assist, and how

First responders and veterans, first. Some 8,700 veterans live in Castle Rock alone — and for many of them, and for the firefighters, medics and officers alongside them, a clinic waiting room is exactly the wrong door. Equine-assisted therapy is the right one: a horse asks for regulation before conversation, honesty before analysis, and presence before anything at all. Lichen’s care model wraps that session in a team — a therapist, the horse, and the coordination that makes the next step obvious — so a first visit becomes a path, not a one-off.

The same doors open to trauma survivors, caregivers running on empty, young people who need a reason to show up somewhere, and families healing together — the people Four Winds has already served for fifteen years. Lichen’s published subsidy policy means ability to pay never decides who gets to stand in the arena.

The scheduling layer — efficiency, and breathing room

The gentle first taste of Lichen’s platform is a calendar that finally matches how a sanctuary actually works. Every room, arena, pasture, animal and practitioner has a real schedule; sessions are booked through stewards who approve each request; and AI helps find the times that work across many organizations without a single spreadsheet or double-booked barn.

Just as important is what the calendar refuses to give away: breathing time. On Lichen, nobody is bookable by default — a horse’s day has a ceiling, a pasture’s recovery is scheduled as firmly as a session, and a practitioner’s rest is protected structurally, not left to their willpower. Maximum efficiency and mandatory rest, in the same system — for the land, the animals, and the people who care for both.

Why Four Winds

Thirty-five acres against the Front Range, between Pikes Peak and Longs Peak. The 1899 Wolfensberger barn, whose acoustics are said to rival Carnegie Hall. Pastures, ponds, ancient pines and dark skies. Horses, llamas, goats and a wild mustang, alongside the deer, elk, fox and owls that were already here. A regenerative farm with agricultural status, an organic garden and greenhouse, lodging and practitioner housing, and fifteen years of programming for veterans, first responders, trauma survivors and people with special needs — plus KAHUBA’s seventeen years of sanctuary work on the same ground. Equine-assisted care needs healed land, seasoned animals, and a community that already knows how to hold people. Four Winds is not a promising site for this work; it is the rare place where the work is already true.

Horses and humans, healing each other on land that can’t be sold out from under the work.

And beneath it all, quietly

There is a deeper layer to Lichen — an economy that counts every contribution, human and otherwise, so the generosity that keeps a place like Four Winds alive is finally visible. It will matter enormously, later, once things get rolling. For now it simply hums underneath, and the curious can read more at lichen.health/about.

Three organisations, one proposition

What weaving in looks like

Andrea, Steve — you’ve each been having a version of this conversation with us separately. This page puts it in one place, because the three pieces only work as one thing.

Four Winds Wholeness Sanctuary has been consciously stewarded for over 140 years — homesteaded in 1882, and cared for since 2011 by Andrea Pomarico, alongside the animals, land and healing lineage that call it home. Andrea is ready for its next chapter, and what we’re proposing doesn’t have a name in the current system: not a single buyer, but a network.

  1. Four Winds — the land. Acres, animals and years of documented stewardship, becoming the first property held by Lichen’s Land Trust. No single owner, ever. Andrea takes her equity out gradually rather than through one mortgaged transaction, funded by the network already aligned with this place.
  2. Audacity Lab — the young people. A think-and-do lab running youth programming out of Four Winds, where growing up alongside land, animals and a working regenerative farm helps young people become new-era adults — learning stewardship, reciprocity and collective intelligence by living them, not reading about them. The land stops being a site Audacity Lab visits and becomes the classroom it holds a permanent stake in.
  3. Lichen — the infrastructure. The legal, business and technology layer that makes the other two durable: the Land Trust that receives what’s given and holds the ground, the Current-cy economy that records every contribution once it’s stewarded — hay, work-trade, animal care, the people who show up — and the network every contributor is woven into rather than left holding a receipt.

Separately, each of these is a good project that depends on someone staying generous. Together they’re a structure: the land can’t be sold out from under the programming, the programming has somewhere permanent to stand, and the contributions that keep both alive are counted rather than forgotten. That’s the part worth backing — not one property, but the first working instance of a pattern built to repeat.

Lichen has already published this doctrine: land in its care operates in full service of community, with value flowing back to the land and the people who tend it. Four Winds would be the first place held in relationship with that framework — not a case study. The actual thing, and the template for what comes after it.

The next step is the simplest one: walk the land together, with all three of us in the same place at the same time, and work out the shape of it there.

Land stewardship, in practice

What weaving in looks like for Four Winds

Four Winds Wholeness Sanctuary has been consciously stewarded for over 140 years — homesteaded in 1882, and cared for since 2011 by Andrea Pomarico, alongside the animals, land and healing lineage that call it home. Andrea is ready for its next chapter, and Lichen is proposing something the current system doesn’t have a name for yet: not a single buyer, but a network. This is where “land as participant, not backdrop” stops being a line on our About page and becomes an address — and the first proof of a pattern built to repeat.

  1. Four Winds — the land itself: acres, animals and years of documented stewardship, becoming the first property held by Lichen’s Land Trust — no single owner, ever. Andrea takes her equity out gradually rather than through one mortgaged transaction, funded by the network already aligned with this place: her own community, people within Gaia’s audience who have the means, and everyone else who wants to be woven into what happens here.
  2. Lichen — the legal, business and technology infrastructure that makes this possible: the Land Trust that receives what's given and holds the land, the Current-cy economy that tracks every contribution once it's stewarded — hay, work-trade, animal care, the people who show up — and the network every donor is woven into, not left holding a receipt. Four Winds is the first proof; the pattern is built to activate a global network of healing spaces, and spaces where goods are made and services are rendered.
  3. Audacity Lab — the educational programming partner: a think-and-do lab running youth programming out of Four Winds, where growing up alongside land, animals and a working regenerative farm helps young people grow into new-era adults — learning stewardship, reciprocity and collective intelligence by living them, not reading about them.
  4. Gaia TV — the research and programming partner running its own field studies and production on the land Lichen stewards — a place to point cameras and instruments at, permanently, rather than for one shoot.

Lichen has already published this doctrine: land in its care operates in full service of community, with value flowing back to the land and the people who tend it. Four Winds would be the first place held in relationship with that framework — not a case study. The actual thing, and the template for what comes after it. Let’s walk the land together and figure out the rest.

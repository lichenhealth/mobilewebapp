The Need-Based Criteria

How Lichen assesses for care — the algorithm, in plain language

Working draft · Numbers pending final approval

Our subsidy policy promises that support is allocated against written, need-based criteria — never by relationship, reputation, or who asked first. This page provides those criteria. They are written and dated before they are applied, and updated here in the open whenever they change.

Where the picture comes from

Everything begins with the Web of Wellbeing (WOW) assessment — a guided walk through six dimensions of health: mental, physical, social, spiritual, environmental, and economic. For each, you describe where you are now and what stands in the way, in your own words — both the inner obstacles (beliefs, feelings, stories) and the outer ones (the real-world circumstances that money alone can’t fix). The inner work belongs to you and your care team; the outer obstacles are what a care coordinator helps you navigate.

The economic dimension gathers monthly income, monthly expenses, assets, and debts — with a focus on how you’re earning your money, how you’re spending it, what is resourcing you and what is depleting you. That financial picture is held privately, visible only to you and your care team, with a lever that gives you permission to omit any (or all) of your WOW information from the support that your AI assistant provides.

Who reads your picture — and what never reads it

Setting a rate is not artificial intelligence. No model reads your words, weighs your story, or scores your worthiness — a human steward does that reading, against these written criteria, and records the reasons. Lichen’s AI simply assists your care team in maximizing your access to the next, best resource on your path of healing and self actualization. You have complete control over what aspects of your WOW you’d like to omit from the AI assistant(s) across the platform. And if you choose not to share the numbers at all, nothing is computed about you — the conversation with your steward simply carries more of the weight.

The Criteria

An income line alone can’t see a life. Two people can earn the same modest amount and be in entirely different places: a teacher — call her Maya — paid poorly for essential work, who needs care no insurance policy covers and quietly funds clean food and activity for her niece and nephew, whose parent can’t — and another member — call them Sam — earning just as little, currently dependent on alcohol and online gambling to temporarily avoid the distress of unresolved PTSD. A single yardstick treats those two lives identically. Our criteria read the whole picture instead:

  • What comes in, and how. Not just the amount, but the work behind it — what it asks of you and what it makes possible.
  • What goes out, and toward what. Spending that resources a life — care, dependents, nourishing food, what keeps a household well — is read as need, not discretion. The support you give to others counts alongside the support you need yourself.
  • What’s left. The real margin between the two, and the assets and debts behind it — the gap a subsidy actually has to close.
  • What care comes next. A subsidy is not only an amount — it is aimed. The right next intervention is different for those two lives: naturopathic care for Maya, addiction support and trauma therapy for Sam. Every subsidy asks the same question: what most reduces the depletion of collective resources, and most restores this person’s capacity to contribute to them.

From that picture, a human steward sets your rate and its aim at enrollment against these written criteria — dated, recorded, with its reasons — and from then on it is automatic: every session subsidized at your rate, with no re-asking and no re-justifying.

How a subsidy actually flows

Practitioners charge what they need to charge. When you book subsidized care, you pay your computed share through Lichen and the fund quietly pays the rest — one payment, nothing to redeem, nothing to prove at the point of care. Your practitioner always receives their full fee and is not told who is subsidized. If Maya’s naturopath charges $140 and her rate is 25%, she pays $35 and the well covers $105.

A human at the door — once

Every request for support gets a real look from a human steward, and every decision — yes or no, the rate, and its aim — is recorded with its reasons against the version of these criteria in force that day. After enrollment, nothing about your subsidy has to be re-earned; if your picture changes, your care team can revisit the rate at any time.

The second door

The criteria above serve the first door of our policy — “I can’t afford it.” The second door, “I never would have tried it,” does not run through the formula: when a Lichen clinician or care coordinator sees a genuine fit for someone who’d never have found their way to it, the community funds the introductory experience in full. Exposure is a resource too.

Limits that keep the well honest

  • Per session: the fund contributes up to $150 toward any single session.
  • Per member: the fund contributes up to $600 per member per month.
  • The hard stop: the fund never spends past what it actually holds. Subsidies pause before the well runs dry; they are never borrowed against tomorrow’s gifts.

These limits exist so one profound need never quietly empties the well for everyone else. Where a real need runs past them, that is a conversation with a human steward, not a bigger automatic draw.

Small on purpose

We err on the side of keeping the community small and supporting the members already in it well, before expanding — fewer people receiving deeper support, growing carefully as the well fills and the platform matures. It is the same reason Lichen is in alpha today: we are testing this with a small group, in the open, and doing it right before the doors open wider.

Never by relationship

Being a donor, or being related to one, neither qualifies nor disqualifies anyone. How someone arrives — referred by a member, a practitioner, or a donor, or finding us on their own — has no bearing on their rate. Subsidies are decided against these written criteria, never by relationship.

Iterating in the open

These criteria are kept as dated, versioned policy — we aim to keep evolving, refining and improving the algorithms and real, human decisions that aid in our mission to rebalance inequities in goods and resources between people, plants, animals, elements and the planet. We are committed to showing our work and humbly remind ourselves and each other that a better society is a work in progress that we’re all committed to turning into a more perfect union within the lichen network.

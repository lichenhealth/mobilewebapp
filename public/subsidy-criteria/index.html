The Need-Based Criteria

How Lichen assesses for care — the algorithm, in plain language

Working draft · Numbers pending final approval

Our subsidy policy promises that support is allocated against written, need-based criteria — never by relationship, reputation, or who asked first. This page is those criteria. They are deliberately plain arithmetic: a formula anyone can check, dated before it is applied, and updated here in the open whenever it changes.

Where the picture comes from

Everything begins with the Web of Wellbeing (WOW) assessment — a guided walk through six dimensions of health: mental, physical, social, spiritual, environmental, and economic. For each, you describe where you are now and what stands in the way, in your own words — both the inner obstacles (beliefs, feelings, stories) and the outer ones (the real-world circumstances that money alone can’t fix). The inner work belongs to you and your care team; the outer obstacles are what a care coordinator helps you navigate.

The economic dimension also gathers four plain numbers — the shape people already know from any application for help: monthly income, monthly expenses, assets, and debts — plus your household size. That financial picture is held privately, visible only to you and your care team, and those numbers are all the subsidy formula ever reads.

What the formula reads — and what it never reads

The subsidy computation is not artificial intelligence. No model reads your words, weighs your story, or scores your worthiness. The formula is arithmetic run on the numbers above, and nothing else — your assessment’s words, your care board, and your conversations never enter it. Any line of your financial profile you mark “omit from AI” is likewise held out of every assistant on the platform. If you choose not to share the numbers at all, no rate can be computed — and a human steward simply works it through with you directly instead.

The tiers

Your rate comes from your household income measured against the federal poverty level for your household size — the same yardstick sliding-scale clinics have used for decades. The dollar thresholds come from the federal guidelines published each year, so they update annually without the criteria themselves changing.

Household incomeYou pay
At or below 150% of the poverty levelNothing — fully subsidized
150% – 200%25% of the practitioner’s fee
200% – 250%50% of the practitioner’s fee
250% – 300%75% of the practitioner’s fee
Above 300%The full fee — no subsidy

The margin adjustment. Income alone can lie about a life. If your monthly income minus your monthly expenses leaves less than $200 of real margin, or your debts far outweigh your assets, your tier moves one step toward more support. The adjustment only ever moves in your favor.

How a subsidy actually flows

Practitioners charge what they need to charge. When you book subsidized care, you pay your computed share through Lichen and the fund quietly pays the rest — one payment, nothing to redeem, nothing to prove at the point of care. Your practitioner always receives their full fee and is not told who is subsidized. If the fee is $140 and your rate is 25%, you pay $35 and the well covers $105.

A human at the door — once

Every request for support gets a real look from a human steward at enrollment, and every decision — yes or no — is recorded with its reasons. After that, the subsidy is automatic: each session is subsidized at your rate with no re-asking and no re-justifying. A steward can correct a rate the formula got wrong about a real life, and every such correction is recorded with its reason, so the file always shows how the criteria were applied.

The second door

The tiers serve the first door of our policy — “I can’t afford it.” The second door, “I never would have tried it,” does not run through the formula: when a Lichen clinician or care coordinator sees a genuine fit for someone who’d never have found their way to it, the community funds the introductory experience in full. Exposure is a resource too.

Limits that keep the well honest

  • Per session: the fund contributes up to $150 toward any single session.
  • Per member: the fund contributes up to $600 per member per month.
  • The hard stop: the fund never spends past what it actually holds. Subsidies pause before the well runs dry; they are never borrowed against tomorrow’s gifts.

These limits exist so one profound need never quietly empties the well for everyone else. Where a real need runs past them, that is a conversation with a human steward, not a bigger automatic draw.

Never by relationship

Being a donor, or being related to one, neither qualifies nor disqualifies anyone. How someone arrives — referred by a member, a practitioner, or a donor, or finding us on their own — has no bearing on their rate. Subsidies are decided against these written criteria, never by relationship.

Versioned in the open

These criteria are kept as dated, versioned policy — the version in force when you enrolled is the one recorded with your enrollment, and any change is published on this page before it applies to anyone. While the banner above reads “working draft,” the percentages and caps here are the proposal under review; the structure — poverty-level tiers, the margin adjustment, a human at the door, hard limits — is settled policy.
